Tim,
Thanks for commenting. You are correct that Keith's is at least the most widely recognized IPA in Canada. That being said, it is nothing like an IPA, either by today's definitions (see the Beer Judging Certification Panel's exhaustive style guide, which doesn't have any corporate interests that I notice) or by the beer that was historically known as India Pale Ale.

Keith's brewery has actually only been in existence since 1820 (which I suppose is nearly \"centuries\"), Alexander himself only immigrating from Scotland in 1817. You may well be thinking of the history of India Pale Ales in England, which were indeed popular with the British living in India early in the British reign.

So why do I identify Keith's as something other then an IPA? Well, ignoring the current guidelines (which are quite clear: it's not), historically India Pale Ales were lighter coloured ales (so far so good) that were brewed specifically to endure the long trip around Africa to India. A trip that could take weeks. Most beer in a wooden casks would long have gone bad by the time it got to India, but beers with higher alcohol seemed to make the trip better. The same was true with beers that had more hops. Not long, and brewers were making higher alcohol beers that had significantly more hops in them solely for shipping to India. So, light coloured beer with higher then usual alcohol and more hops. At 5% abv and under 20IBU (which is a measurement of bitterness), currently Keith's IPA just doesn't measure up, to either the historic or current standards.

Of course, their original/older product may well have more closely represented the style. It could be that they have been dumbing it down for decades to keep up with a market that (despite it's assurances to the contrary), simply doesn't like beer.

In doing some fact checking, I came across this blog with interesting images.
http://barclayperkins.blogspot.com/2009/05/beer-in-1890s-canada.html
Note in table one the top two beers are Keith's Ale and Keith's XXX, both of which weigh-in at over 7% (though a true beer geek notes the Ale would actually have had to be 6.1% given the gravities listed: http://www.bartowel.com/board/viewtopic.php?p=55337). Also note that they only list \"Ale\", not \"India Pale Ale\" which does appear a variety of times further down the tables. Also note that aside from the Labatt IPA, all the others are above 6% abv. Histroically, the evidence is that IPAs were higher alcohol. In fact, even the Bass & Co. Pale Ales were above 6%.

I would be a very happy beer drinker indeed, if Keith's decided to rebrand IPA to \"Ale\" or \"Export Ale\" or even \"Cream Ale\" (though I wouldn't love the last one). So often somebody comes into the bar I work at, and asks for a Keith's. When we tell them we don't have it, and recommend Moosehead, they sometimes spot a bottle of IPA and ask for that. We try to explain that it is nothing like Keith's, and most of the time they take our word for it, though occasionally they don't, and those instances generally end in a nearly full bottle of IPA getting poured out.

Bless your Uncle Archie, that's a great story. I'm guessing the reference to Indiana is probably a dig at American beers being dumbed-down versions of their European counter parts (which in today's beer market, is totally untrue. Yes, the top sellers might be, but the USA is kicking Canada's ass all over the map when it comes to brewing these days).

Thanks again for commenting, if you're interested in reading some reviews of IPAs, check out http://www.torontobeerblog.com/tag/ipa/

Chris

So, if you've been following along, last night I broke into a fancy-schmancy wooden box that was sent to me by Keith's.  There was a sexy combination lock with a note that said I would get the combination later, and in the meantime I should refridgerate the box.  I'm impatient, and in possesion of numerous tools including a screwdriver, so I simply took the locking hinge off.  Being questionable sober last night, I decided to wait till today to let it cool and have a clear(ish) mind to give it a go.

I'll be honest, Keith's underwhelms me, generally speaking.  That being said, their White is a decent option at many pubs that are essentially beer wastelands.  And I'm aware that I'm a bit more than a bit of a beer snob.  Keith's has been the gateway for many people I know, jumping from macro to micro drinkers, so they still have my respect.  If we could get them to re-brand their India Pale Ale to just Ale, I would even call myself a fan.  So let's see where we stand after this new seasonal.

[caption id="attachment_812" align="aligncenter" width="400" caption="Alexander Keith's Harvest Ale"][/caption]

From an ISB with no freshness date (mind you, this is not a retail bottle) the beer pours a clear dark reddish-brown, with a very active head that drops to about an inch that hangs around.  Aroma is dark malts, a touch of wood and a bit of earthy hops.  Not a bad start.  Taste is hoppier then the aroma would have suggested.  Biscuity malts get cut pretty quickly by a moderate earthy hop.  Mouthfeel is a little on the thin side; I'd like a little more weight from the malt.  But it's quite drinkable and nice.  I'm actually really impressed.  Certianly, this is not a stand-out brew in the wider world of craft beer, but coming from such a large brewery I'm honestly really happy with the product.  This is a beer that could open a lot of people's minds, and I'm all for that.  I know the legions of high-end snobs will be calling for my inverted crucifixion for daring to lend my voice to a Labatt product, but seriously, I adore Creemore Kellerbeir; I have no problem taking an objective sip and finding a Keith's product that I genuinely like.

Thanks Alexander Keith's, now let's just work on that IPA rebrand.......
